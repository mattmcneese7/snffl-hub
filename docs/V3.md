# Squirtnite FFL Hub: v3.0

Agreed with Matt on 28 September 2026. `docs/BRIEF.md` still governs everything
it covers; this extends it. Same rules apply: one checkpoint at a time, scope
and a token estimate before each, and nothing built that is not described here.

---

## 1. What v3.0 is for

v2 made the league legible: scores, the Rag, highlights, the week in ninety
seconds. It is a thing you read.

v3 makes it a thing you act in, and a thing that knows something. Five
strands:

1. **Numbers that are right for this league**, not for a generic one.
2. **A statistics spine**, because the data is free and we take one field from
   it today.
3. **The Swami**, which turns that spine into something worth reading.
4. **Movement**, so the league sees itself moving in real time rather than
   finding out on Tuesday.
5. **Acting from here**, so the app is where a lineup gets set.

---

## 2. The projection finding

This is the headline, because it is a bug rather than a modelling gap and it
has been wrong all season.

`lib/projections.ts` already pulls Sleeper's own projections from
`api.sleeper.com/projections/nfl`. It then takes the precomputed `pts_ppr`
field. That field is scored on **Sleeper's default rules**, and this league's
rules are not the defaults:

| Category | Sleeper default | Squirtnite |
|---|---|---|
| Interception thrown | -1 | **-2** |
| Shutout | 10 | **5** |
| 1 to 6 allowed | 7 | **4** |
| 7 to 13 allowed | 4 | **3** |
| Yards allowed | not priced | **+5 to -7** |
| FG 50 to 59 | lumped at 5 | **5**, with 60+ at **6** |

**Measured, once the engine existed**, as the mean change to a week's
projections:

| | Change per player week | Worst single case |
|---|---|---|
| QB | **-0.66** | -1.15 |
| DEF | **-0.48** | -2.94 |
| K | **-0.21** | -0.44 |
| RB, WR, TE | 0.02, which is rounding | - |

The interception line is a correction to this document's first draft, which
claimed skill players were unaffected because the league was otherwise
standard. It is not: interceptions cost double here, and a starting
quarterback is projected for about two thirds of one a week, so every QB
projection ran about 0.66 too high. Backs, receivers and tight ends genuinely
are unaffected.

**The fix is not a new model.** It is to compute points from the raw projected
`stats` multiplied by this league's own `scoring_settings`, both of which we
already fetch. Verified present in the DEF payload: `pts_allow`, the
points-allowed bucket flags, `sack`, `int`, `def_td`, `fum_rec`, `ff`,
`blk_kick`, `def_kr_yd`, `def_pr_yd`.

Two categories the league scores are not projected by Sleeper: `def_st_ff` and
`def_st_fum_rec`, worth 1.0 each. They get approximated from the team level
`ff` and `fum_rec` with the assumption documented, because a special teams
forced fumble is a small fraction of a defense's weekly total and inventing
precision there would be worse than admitting the gap.

**What the fix is worth, honestly.** Mean absolute error against actual points
barely moves: 0.10 for QB, 0.06 for DEF, 0.01 for K, nothing for the rest.
That is not a disappointing result, it is the expected one, and it is worth
being clear about why. Projection error is dominated by football, not by
arithmetic: a quarterback's week is 8 points of error wide, so removing a 0.66
bias is a rounding adjustment against it.

The value is that the bias was **systematic and one directional**. Every QB and
every defence was inflated, every week, and a nine man starting lineup stacked
those errors in the same direction rather than cancelling them out. Win
probability, playoff odds, the pace line and Squirt Says all read these
numbers, and a consistent lean is worth more to them than a per player average
suggests.

**This lands first.** Start/sit, the trade evaluator and the movement feed's
"points left on the bench" all read these numbers, and defence versus position
in Checkpoint 13 is built by scoring real stat lines with this same engine.
Fixing it last would mean building four features on a foundation known to be
wrong.

## 3. Identity

Sleeper has no OAuth and no authentication of any kind: confirmed again on 28
September 2026, in their words, "We do not perform authentication as our API is
read-only". That does not stop the app having accounts. It just means the
accounts are ours.

Sign in with Google or Apple through Supabase Auth, which is already in the
stack. A person links to their roster once, and from then on their settings,
their alerts, their team and their BOOM Roasted submissions follow them rather
than the browser they happened to use. The current "pick your team, remembered
on this device" becomes the fallback for anyone who does not sign in.

## 4. Movement

Nobody publishes a lineup change feed. We manufacture one.

A poller snapshots each roster's `starters` and player list on a schedule and
diffs consecutive snapshots. The diff is the feed: who benched whom, who
dropped a player hours before he went off, who has not touched a lineup since
Thursday. `/league/{id}/transactions/{week}` supplies trades, waivers and free
agent moves alongside it.

This needs no credentials and nothing private. It is the same public read the
app already does, kept over time, which is the only thing Sleeper's API does
not do for you.

Falls out of it for free:

- **Points left on the bench.** Bench actuals against the lineup that started.
- **The shame board.** Who set theirs last, which feeds BOOM Roasted.
- **Lineup alerts.** On the push system that already exists.

## 5. Acting from here

**This supersedes the September 2026 decision in `DECISIONS.md`**, which
concluded that deep links into Sleeper were the finished feature. Matt's call
on 28 September 2026, made with the risks below stated, is that the app should
set lineups and propose trades directly.

### What it requires

Sleeper's public API cannot do this. The Sleeper web app talks to a private
authenticated API, so the app would hold a session for each manager who opts
in, and drive that API on their behalf.

### The risks, stated once, in writing

- **Accounts can be flagged or banned.** The blast radius lands on league
  members' Sleeper accounts, not on this project.
- **We become custodians of credentials.** A session token is a live key to
  somebody's account.
- **It is unsupported.** A private API can change without notice and take the
  feature with it, mid-season.

### The rules the build follows

- **No password is ever stored.** A session token only, encrypted at rest,
  in Supabase, never in the repo and never in a client bundle.
- **Opt in, per manager, revocable.** Nobody is enrolled by being in the
  league. Revoking deletes the token.
- **The client is honest.** It identifies itself and respects documented rate
  limits.
- **No anti-detection engineering.** No rotating user agents, no randomised
  human-like timing, no proxy rotation. Automating accounts we are entitled to
  use is the goal; defeating a system built to stop abuse is not, and it is
  also what turns a quiet tool into a bannable one.

### Go or no go

Discovery and the prototype run against **Matt's account only**. Nothing
touches another manager's account until Matt has seen it work and decided to
take it league wide. That gate is a checkpoint boundary, not a note.

## 6. The Swami

The deepest strand, and the one with the most headroom, because the data is
sitting there unused. nflverse is already a dependency and we take exactly one
thing from it: a Sleeper to ESPN id map. It also publishes weekly player stats
and snap counts, free, which is the whole spine of real analysis.

### The stats spine

Three things computed once a week and stored, none of which exist today:

- **Defense versus position.** Fantasy points each NFL defense concedes to each
  position, scored on **this league's rules** rather than generic ones, which
  matters for exactly the reason section 2 does.
- **Usage.** Snap share, target share, carry share, red zone looks, and the
  trend across the last three weeks. Usage is what separates a player having a
  quiet week from a player losing his job.
- **Schedule ahead.** Which defenses every rostered player faces over the next
  few weeks, joined to defense versus position.

Weekly aggregates and snap counts only, not full play by play. A season of play
by play is tens of megabytes and none of it is needed to answer the questions
above.

### What it produces

- **Matchup aware projections.** Sleeper's number adjusted for the defense the
  player actually faces. A floor and a ceiling rather than one figure.
- **Player pages with something to say.** Usage trend, role, what the next
  three matchups look like, whether the last quiet week was a matchup or a
  demotion.
- **Matchup implications.** Which two or three players actually swing a fantasy
  matchup, and what has to happen for the underdog to win it.

### The voice

`lib/squirt-says.ts` is already the right shape and the rule it follows is the
one the Swami inherits: *every saying is a real number with a sentence wrapped
around it, and the number is always shown, so nobody has to take the oracle's
word for it.* The Swami is that, widened from one lineup to the whole league
and forward across the schedule.

Deterministic where the arithmetic decides the sentence, which is most of it
and costs nothing to run. Claude only where genuine synthesis across the league
is the point, with the underlying numbers printed next to whatever it says, so
a wrong read is visible rather than authoritative.

---

## 7. ESPN

Read only, no credentials, no accounts. Injury designations, practice
participation and news blurbs, surfaced where a lineup decision is being made:
a starter marked questionable who did not practise Wednesday should say so on
the screen where you are choosing him.

ESPN fantasy leagues are explicitly out of scope. The league lives on Sleeper.

## 8. Checkpoints

| # | Checkpoint | Output | Est. tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | **Projections on this rulebook** | Points computed from raw stats times the league's own scoring settings. A backtest over the season so far reporting mean absolute error before and after, per position. | 12K to 18K |
| 13 | **The stats spine** | nflverse weekly stats and snap counts ingested nightly. Defense versus position on this league's scoring, usage and trend, schedule ahead. Stored, not recomputed per request. | 25K to 35K |
| 14 | **Matchup aware projections** | Sleeper's number adjusted for the defense faced, with a floor and a ceiling. Player pages carrying usage, role and the next three matchups. ESPN injury and practice status alongside. | 25K to 35K |
| 15 | **The Swami** | Synthesis across the league and the slate, extending Squirt Says. Matchup implications, who swings a game, what the underdog needs. Numbers printed beside every claim. | 25K to 35K |
| 16 | **Movement** | Snapshot and diff poller, the movement feed, points left on the bench, the shame board, lineup alerts. | 25K to 35K |
| 17 | **Identity** | Supabase auth with Google and Apple, roster linking, per-device state migrated to the account. | 20K to 28K |
| 18 | **Decisions** | Start and sit on the corrected numbers, trade evaluator on rest of season value, waiver suggestions. | 20K to 30K |
| 19 | **Write path: discovery and prototype** | The private API surface documented, a single account prototype against Matt's roster, encrypted token storage. **Ends at a go or no go.** | 20K to 30K |
| 20 | **Write path: league wide** | Opt in flow, revocation, set a lineup and propose a trade from the app. Only if 19 passes. | 25K to 35K |

**Estimated total:** about 207K to 281K output tokens. Revisions historically
add about half again, so plan on 310K to 420K.

**The order is not arbitrary.** 12 corrects numbers that 13 through 18 all
read. 13 is the spine 14 and 15 stand on. The write path sits last because it
is the only strand that can fail for reasons outside the code, and everything
else should already be shipped and useful before it is attempted.
